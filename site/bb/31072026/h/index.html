The Take

I was asked this week: How do you measure the value of the outcomes of AI and I answered like this; Capability.

And I not only love this question and answer - but that it creates a quick understanding of why we don't really TRUST our business operations to this kind of technology.

Because if your team can't seem to get something working, you might start building it with Claude Code - or Gemini or some tooling. And if you get it working and miraculously it provides additional data... CONGRATULATIONS!

You've now made what I'm coining "Capability DEBT" - that is - a false capability to provide something.

Stick with me here. A team can't seem to get a CRM to launch on their sales and marketing teams - they've tried the small ones, the big ones - and even hired a partner to get them going. Nothing.

So someone passionate on the team - someone who literally NEEDS this capability (say VP of sales) starts building one with Claude Code. He even does it securely and it gets so good that his team wants access.

Well - naturally we go to IT next to get this thing into production right?

"Well, we WISH we could, but your application has over 10,000 lines of code and we found 16 errors in the first 100 lines."

And then, the project stalls. The VP is running their own system. Maybe another rep launches their own personal one - maybe they are even sending personal data between their instances.

Now - I know I'm talking about Shadow AI, which is a problem - but more importantly you've created CAPABILITY DEBT - a capability which you achieve through inexpensive, simple work; without laying the foundation of good data, good process, good governance and GREAT system design.

What's terrible about this scenario is that you KNOW that VP of sales is willing to do ANYTHING to get their numbers - so if you can't take this capability on and build it the right way - you KNOW that shadow AI is right there tempting all your best teams.

So what's the lesson here?

Fit-for-purpose solutions are great AND if you're not going to chose a solution that is designed to work well, you MUST be prepared for the pressure this creates in Demand as well as Security.

What do you think? Will this kind of (lack of) innovation drive the best talent out of organizations? Will IT get better at ingesting new custom apps from the business? Will the business become their own IT departments?

Everything you need to know. Nothing you don't.

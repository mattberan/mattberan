Someone pointed out last week, an organization they were a part of failed because the wrong people were in charge; which led to this awesome, awesome question... are the right people in charge?

And that got me thinking more about tactical leaders, strategic leaders and balanced leaders. How leaders go through phrases and organizations and teams have different need that change differently FROM those leaders, what they can offer and what kind of teams they can lead.

If an organization can flex, move, hire and purge talent quickly in and out of these positions in a fluid like manner; major productivity and efficiency cheat code.

And when I write that, I think of the successful agile teams I was and how it felt like we were all the right people for the job, and when we weren't - the next person would jump in (with a handoff of sorts).

I think what I'm getting at is that there may be an "ideal" way of working; that with the right combination of skills, talents, personalities and leadership; we actually have a fighting chance at success.

But what most people focus on is "having an incredible idea" or "competitive advantage" or "corporate strategy".

They don't seem to get so excited about team chemistry, mental health or discussions about balance.

I *require* the second type of team now, the first? I'm not intrested in; because they might as well be AI.

There is a lack of humanity in an un-caring, faceless organization.

Theory: When the business interactions become stale, cheap and inhumane, they lose most of their value.

What do you think? Are the right people in charge where you are? Do you think they would be offended or welcome the question?
